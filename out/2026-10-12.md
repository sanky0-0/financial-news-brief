**Why this matters:**
- A stronger dollar directly pressures multinationals with high overseas sales — Apple (AAPL) generates ~60% of revenue internationally, Microsoft (MSFT) ~48%, potentially trimming Q3 earnings by 2-3% on forex headwinds.
- Rising oil (+WTI up 8% in 3 weeks) inflates jet fuel costs, squeezing margins at Delta (DAL) and United (UAL); every $1/bbl sustained increase adds ~$200M in annual fuel cost for the Big 3 US carriers.
- The dollar-oil feedback loop complicates Fed policy — sticky energy inflation delays rate cuts, hitting interest-sensitive sectors like homebuilders (D.R. Horton (DHI), Lennar (LEN)) where mortgage rates may stay above 7%.
- Conversely, US-focused energy producers (ExxonMobil (XOM), Pioneer Natural (PXD)) benefit from higher crude while repatriated foreign earnings gain a currency tailwind.

### Geopolitics
**US and UK hold phone talks on Iran, confirm importance of accountability** — [→ this.kiji.is](https://news.jp/i/1482471897249776374)
**Over 30 countries, including UK, US, Europe, condemn Iran over terror plot case in statement** — [→ this.kiji.is](https://news.jp/i/1482471011732635803)

**Why this matters:**
- Coordinated US–UK–EU condemnation and the call for “accountability” signal a high probability of fresh sanctions on Iranian oil exports, which currently supply ~1.5 million barrels per day to global markets. Any disruption tightens supply and pushes Brent crude above $90/bbl — directly benefiting energy majors like **ExxonMobil (XOM)** and **Chevron (CVX)**, while squeezing fuel costs for airlines such as **Delta (DAL)** and shipping giant **A.P. Moller–Maersk (AMKBY)**.
- Escalated geopolitical risk in the Middle East typically drives a bid for defense stocks. Look for potential upside in prime contractors with missile‑defense and surveilance exposure, including **Lockheed Martin (LMT)** and **RTX (RTX)**, as allied governments accelerate procurement.
- The Strait of Hormuz, through which ~20% of the world’s oil passes, becomes a flashpoint. Any naval incidents or mine‑laying would spike insurance rates for tankers, hitting shipping names like **Frontline (FRO)** positively (higher day rates) but raising costs for crude importers — watch refiner margins at **Valero (VLO)** and **Philips 66 (PSX)** if crude differentials widen sharply.
