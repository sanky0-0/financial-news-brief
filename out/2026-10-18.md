South Korea’s Finance Minister Choi Sang-mok made the day’s boldest call, forecasting the semiconductor boom will run until 2027—a signal to keep riding AI-fueled chip demand through names like Samsung Electronics (005930.KS) and SK Hynix (000660.KS). Today matters because it juxtaposes secular tech optimism with an energy gambit that is about volumes, not employment, while handing traders a discrete event to price into a Saudi small cap.

### Semiconductors
**South Korea's Finance Minister Forecasts Semiconductor Boom to Last Until 2027** — [→ gurufocus.com](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9129055/south-koreas-finance-minister-forecasts-semiconductor-boom-to-last-until-2027)

**Why this matters:**
- Samsung (005930.KS) and SK Hynix (000660.KS) — which together command ~70% of the global memory market — can sustain elevated pricing and margins through 2027, likely rewarding shareholders with higher dividends and buybacks; watch for guidance upgrades at next earnings.
- AI-driven demand for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) is the fuel: NVIDIA (NVDA) and AMD (AMD) are booking HBM3E supply years in advance, so any hiccup in Samsung’s or SK Hynix’s yield ramps could push lead times out past 52 weeks, directly impacting GPU delivery schedules.
- Micron (MU) stands to benefit from the spillover, as a multi-year boom validates its aggressive HBM roadmap and could accelerate its planned $150B capex cycle, lifting the entire memory equipment supply chain (ASML (ASML), Lam Research (LRCX)).
