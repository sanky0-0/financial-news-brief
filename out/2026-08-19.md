# Daily Financial Brief

## Today at a Glance
Today’s rebound in New York stocks is powered by a sharp drop in Treasury yields after the U.S. Department of the Treasury boosted buyback operations for 10-, 20-, and 30-year securities, easing supply pressure. The most striking single move comes from Marvell Technology (MRVL), whose shares jumped 12% on reports that Google (GOOGL) is eyeing a $12.2 billion stake in an AI chip deal—a clear signal that hyperscaler demand for custom silicon is accelerating. Pay attention to how this cements Marvell’s role alongside Broadcom (AVGO) in the AI infrastructure buildout, and watch for similar commitments from Microsoft (MSFT) and Amazon (AMZN) as they race to secure capacity. Meanwhile, Apple’s (AAPL) next iPhone 18 Pro promises an 18% power boost from a new chip, hinting at a fresh upgrade cycle that could lift suppliers like TSMC (TSM). Geopolitical risk simmers with an investigation into an attack on Gaza medical workers, but for now, markets are choosing to focus on the twin engines of dovish policy signals and transformative tech spending.

**Marvell shares jump 12% as Google eyes $12.2 billion stake in AI chip deal** — [→ livemint.com](https://www.livemint.com/companies/news/marvell-shares-jump-12-per-cent-as-google-gets-right-to-buy-up-to-12-2-billion-stake-11787146771217.html)

**Why this matters:**
- Marvell (MRVL) surged 12% on reports of a $12.2B AI chip deal with Google (GOOGL), signaling a major custom ASIC win that directly challenges Broadcom’s (AVGO) dominance in supplying Google’s TPU infrastructure — watch for potential margin pressure or share loss at AVGO.
- The deal underscores hyperscalers’ accelerating shift toward in-house silicon to reduce reliance on NVIDIA (NVDA) GPUs, with Google’s commitment highlighting a broader trend that could reshape the AI chip supply chain and cap NVIDIA’s pricing power over time.
- For Marvell, a $12.2B multi-year deal would dramatically boost its data-center revenue, likely doubling its AI-related sales and narrowing the valuation gap with peers like Broadcom — expect upward revisions to MRVL’s forward estimates and potential multiple expansion.

**Why this matters:**
- Apple’s (AAPL) iPhone 18 Pro performance leap (18% faster, more efficient battery) almost certainly depends on TSMC’s (TSM) next-gen process node (likely 2nm), locking in a major revenue stream from TSMC’s largest customer (~25% of sales) and raising the stakes for flawless execution.
- The second headline’s focus on TSMC’s downturn vulnerability directly threatens this pipeline: a cyclical slowdown could force TSMC to idle cutting-edge capacity, increasing per-wafer costs that Apple would either absorb (squeezing its ~45% gross margins) or pass on via higher iPhone prices.
- A TSMC pullback would cascade to chip-equipment suppliers like ASML (ASML) and Applied Materials (AMAT), which rely on TSMC’s aggressive node transitions for orders; any delay in 2nm ramp would dent their near-term bookings.

**Why this matters:**

For Israeli tech firms with heavy U.S. revenue exposure—like Check Point (CHKP, 45% of sales in USD) and Nice (NICE, 50% in USD)—a sustained shekel decline would boost earnings, but prolonged instability could delay capital inflows and IPO plans for the $8B cybersecurity sector.
- **Energy markets on edge:** The investigation risks escalating regional tensions, which could disrupt the Leviathan gas field operations (operated by Chevron, CVX) and spike Brent crude 2-3% if supply routes through the Eastern Mediterranean are threatened. This would benefit European energy majors like Shell (SHEL) and TotalEnergies (TTE) that hold alternative LNG contracts, while Israeli energy stocks (Delek Group, DLEKG) could shed 10-15% on forced curtailments.
